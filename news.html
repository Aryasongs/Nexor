Nexor Sparks Launches as a Bold New Contender in the Social Media Race

A sleek, real-time "Pulse" sharing app built by a two-person founding team is betting that speed, verification, and a distinct visual identity can carve out space in a crowded market.

By TechWire Staff · Startups Desk
Published 06:12 IST · Updated moments ago · 4 min read
The Nexor Sparks logo. The app markets itself with the tagline: "Share your pulse with the world." — Photo: Nexor

A new name has entered the social media conversation. Nexor Sparks, a mobile-first platform built around short, real-time updates the app calls "Pulses," has quietly launched with a striking dark, cyan-lit interface and a feature set that borrows the best ideas from the platforms that came before it — while trying to carve its own identity in an increasingly saturated field.

The app allows users to post text, images, and live updates, follow other accounts, react in real time, and message directly within the platform. It also includes a verification system, organisation badges for brand and company accounts, and an AI-content label — a small but telling detail that suggests the team is already thinking about how synthetic media will reshape social platforms in the years ahead.

What has caught early attention, however, isn't just the feature list. It's the team behind it.

"We didn't want to build another feed. We wanted to build a pulse — something that feels alive."

Nexor was founded by Gursharan Singh, who serves as the company's Chief Executive Officer, alongside co-founder Anmol Sunda. The pair make up the core team steering the platform's early direction, product design, and technical build-out — a lean structure increasingly common among ambitious app-first startups looking to move fast before scaling their teams.

Industry watchers note that the timing is notable. Social platforms have faced growing scrutiny over authenticity, moderation, and AI-generated content, and newer entrants are increasingly expected to address those questions from day one rather than retrofit solutions later. Nexor's early build already reflects some of that thinking, with verification tiers and content labelling built into the core experience rather than bolted on.

“
Every platform starts small. What matters is whether people feel something when they open it. That's what we're building toward with Sparks.
— Gursharan Singh, Founder & CEO, Nexor
What Comes Next

For now, Nexor Sparks remains an early-stage product, and like any new platform, its long-term prospects will depend on the two things every social app ultimately lives or dies by: whether people show up, and whether they stay. The founding team has kept its early messaging modest, framing the launch less as a finished product and more as an invitation — a first pulse in what they hope becomes a much larger network.

TechWire will continue to follow Nexor's progress as the platform develops.
