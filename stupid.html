Dozera News Folds Into Nexor News After Founder's Son Engineers Buyout

A once-independent voice inside the Dozera Group has been absorbed into a rival outlet run by its own founder's son — a deal insiders are calling either a savvy consolidation or a quiet family betrayal, depending on who you ask.

By Alden Marsh, Media & Mergers Correspondent Business Times US.CA Updated 6:42 AM ET 6 min read

Dozera News, for years the media arm of the diversified Dozera Group, is being folded into Nexor News, according to a deal confirmed late Monday that has sent a jolt through the small but fiercely competitive world of independently owned business press. The buyer is not a private equity fund or a distant conglomerate. It is Gursharan Singh — known publicly by his stage name, Gursharn Arya — the son of Dozera Group founder Gurdiyal Singh, who built Nexor News as his own venture and is now using it to swallow the outlet his father spent a decade building.

Terms of the transaction were not fully disclosed, but two people close to the deal, speaking on condition of anonymity because the agreement has not been formally announced to staff, said the purchase price was funded largely through Gursharan Singh's personal holding company rather than Nexor News' balance sheet — a structure that lets him control the outcome without answering to Nexor News' other investors.

Dozera News will not survive as a standalone brand. Its reporters, its archive, and what remains of its subscriber base are being routed directly into Nexor News, whose leadership says the combined newsroom will operate under a single editorial banner by the end of the quarter.

"This isn't a merger of equals. It's a son buying his father's newsroom and renaming it after himself." — Former Dozera News editor, speaking anonymously

The Case For It: Scale, Survival, and a Second Life

Supporters of the deal, including several Nexor News staffers, argue that Dozera News was quietly dying long before Gursharan Singh made his move. Ad revenue at the outlet had reportedly slipped for three straight years, and its parent group had shown little appetite to keep subsidizing a division that no longer moved the needle on the Dozera Group's core businesses.

Nexor News, by contrast, arrives with real momentum: a faster-growing readership, a modern publishing stack, and — crucially — a willingness to actually invest in the newsroom it's inheriting. Several Dozera News journalists have already been offered retained roles, and Nexor News has pledged to preserve the outlet's flagship investigative unit rather than cut it for cost savings, a rare commitment in an industry known for gutting acquired newsrooms.

What's earning praise

  • Editorial jobs largely preserved rather than eliminated in the short term
  • Investigative desk kept intact, unusual for a distressed-asset acquisition
  • Combined outlet gains real reach and a stronger revenue base overnight
  • Ends years of underinvestment that had left Dozera News stagnant

What's drawing criticism

  • A founder's son buying and dissolving his own father's outlet reads, to some, as opportunism dressed up as strategy
  • Deal structured through personal holdings, sidestepping Nexor News' own investor scrutiny
  • Dozera News' independent brand and archive effectively disappear
  • Employees learned key details from reporters, not from management, before any internal announcement

The Case Against It: A Family Company, Sold From the Inside

Not everyone is applauding. Critics inside and outside the Dozera Group describe the deal as, at best, awkward and, at worst, a calculated move by Gursharan Singh to fold a competitor into his own empire by leaning on his father's legacy and standing within the Dozera Group. The optics are difficult to ignore: a son acquiring the media division his father built, then erasing its identity within months of taking control.

Some former Dozera News staff have gone further, alleging that the outlet's decline was allowed to happen — even quietly encouraged — precisely because it made the eventual buyout cheaper. Nexor News has firmly denied any suggestion that Gursharan Singh influenced Dozera News' finances ahead of the deal, calling the claims "unfounded and disconnected from how the negotiation actually unfolded."

"Bringing Dozera News into Nexor News protects the journalism and the jobs behind it. Anything else is speculation." — Spokesperson, Nexor News

Governance experts say the deal raises broader questions about how family-linked media holdings should be scrutinized, even when — as here — no laws appear to have been broken. "When a buyer and a target share a bloodline, the burden of proof on 'arm's length' goes up, not down," said one M&A attorney contacted by Business Times, who was not involved in the transaction. "That doesn't make it improper. It makes it a deal that deserves a harder look than a routine acquisition would."

What Happens Next

Dozera News' website is expected to redirect to Nexor News within the next business cycle, with select archived reporting migrated over and the rest retired. Dozera Group has issued only a brief statement acknowledging the sale, wishing the "next chapter of the newsroom's work" well without directly addressing the family dynamics driving the story. Gursharan Singh has not personally commented beyond the joint release.

For now, the newsroom itself is left holding both halves of the story at once — grateful for a lifeline, and uneasy about exactly whose hand is offering it.
